# Reach: the next two steps

Not a vendor pitch. The app stays free, unnamed as anyone's product, and useful on any eight-bar xylophone. A coffee jar can exist. It is not the product.

Do this before any letter to a toy company. A letter without parents and one teacher is a request for sympathy.

## The options sheet

There is no separate parent website. One sheet, opened from an Options button, closed the same way. It is for an adult. It stays shut while the notes are falling and while a teacher has the phone on the table.

What it is for: settings a child should not tap in the middle of a song, and the only place money is mentioned.

What is on it, in this order:

1. Instrument size. Eight bars, or the larger one. This is the reason the sheet exists.
2. Support, behind a fox. The sheet shows a small fox with a heart, and beside it a plain sentence: the songs stay free, and this is how to treat the fox. The amounts stay hidden until that fox is tapped. Three set amounts, then crypto as a second choice. One tap would open a card payment or copy an address. Until those are live, the tap says it is not open yet. If you have not set up a payment link or an address, the fox is hidden.

Language stays in the header. The song list stays where it is. Nothing else needs a parent page.

The playing screen never shows this sheet, the word support, or a wallet.

## What has to be true first

A kindergarten teacher will try three songs they already sing. If a verse is missing or the melody is the wrong tune, they will not try a fourth. The starter book has to be complete, and right, before the first visit.

## Step 1 — Free, findable, and a jar that does not beg

Show the songs. Do not gate them. The jar is the Support block inside Options. It is not a banner, not a popup, and not on the bars.

Where people can see it, and how. One of each, not a campaign:

- A parent group you are actually in (a kerület group, or óvodás szülők). One post, in Hungarian: what the page does, the link, and the short clip. Say you made it. Do not post the same text in ten groups.
- The clip itself on one short-video place (Reels, TikTok, or YouTube). The picture is a real eight-bar toy beside the phone, notes falling in the same colors. The link sits under it. Do not buy ads.
- One project on LinkedIn: the name, the link, two sentences. That is so a person who searches your name finds the toy. Parents do not live there. No DeFi.

Do not put the link under a shop's photos, and do not review your own page on their site.

What "findable" means for the first month: a parent who already owns a xylophone can open the link and start Boci without an account, a download, or a payment.

What you write down, and how. The app's listen mode is the wrong tool. It misses notes even on a decent phone, so a score on screen is not evidence that a child finished anything. Do not collect these three things with the microphone, and do not collect them from IP addresses.

- Did the child finish a song. You watch, and you write it in a notebook. Friends and neighbors count. Fifteen to thirty notes are enough.
- Did the adult ask for the link again. You hear it, or they write to you. A visit counter can later say the page was opened, without knowing who. That number is not "this parent came back."
- Which song they asked for that was not in the box. Yes: what they type into search, including when the search fails, and what they say out loud in the room. The private song list stores the typed title. You do not need a form.

## Step 2 — One teacher, then a second

When: after those verses are complete, and after Options is the only place money appears. Not as a mailing to every kindergarten.

Do not walk in at drop-off or pickup. The door is busy, and a stranger with a phone looks wrong. Ask a parent you know to introduce you, or call the óvoda and ask for ten minutes with the teacher who does the music circle. Mid-morning, on a week they are not rehearsing a show. The weeks before they pick songs for a small celebration are when they care. The morning of the celebration, they will not talk.

What you say to the person at the door, in Hungarian, and then you stop talking:

"István vagyok. Egy ingyenes oldalt csináltam az óvodás dalokhoz, xilofonra. Nem árulok semmit. Tíz percet kérnék a pedagógustól, amikor éppen nem a gyerekekkel van."

If they say no, leave the paper and go.

Who: that one teacher. The second teacher comes only if the first one uses it again without you in the room.

How the visit goes:

- You bring nothing to install. The link opens on their phone or on one shared phone.
- If they have no xylophone in the room, the phone is the instrument for this look. You tap the bars. You say a wooden one at home, or the one the group already has, can follow the colors later. You do not need their toy to show the songs.
- You do not present. You ask which three songs they already do, and you open those.
- If a song is missing, you write the title down. You do not promise the next version in the room.
- You leave a small paper: the name, the link, and those three titles. No price, no wallet.
- The only question at the end: would you open this again next week.

Come back once, for a sentence. Then ask in words, if it was useful: may I say that you use it, and may I name the óvoda. A nod that they liked it is not that permission. If they hesitate, you may say "an óvoda teacher in Budapest" and nothing more. Do not put their name or the kindergarten's name in a letter to a company without the yes.

What you do not do: pitch a partnership, mention a shop brand, pass around a crypto address, or record children. If a teacher wants a song added, the title can be written down. A child's voice is not collected. If they ask for a song that is still in copyright, you say you can only add traditional ones.

## What the app has to change so these two steps work

- Playing hides the song list already. It must also hide money. Money lives only in Options, as Support.
- Options is that sheet. The first control is the larger xylophone. Support is the second.
- Starter songs finished out to the verses teachers sing, and more traditional songs in the box so search is not the way a teacher finds Boci.
- Search that can return a public-domain tune and keep more than one short verse. A title that is typed and not found is still kept for you.
- Every song an adult types or that search returns is kept for you, quietly. Not shown to other visitors until you have checked it.
- The classroom screen is the normal screen with Options left closed.

## What these steps are not

They are not the vendor conversation. Later, the ask is a QR on one product for one season, not a sale of the app. Exclusivity is not offered.

The QR is your link with a tag on the end, such as `?from=` and a short name for that shop. You can see how many people opened the page from that code. You still cannot see who they are. The shop does not pay you per visit.

Money from a test that went well is a fee, asked afterwards: keep the code on the page for the next season, or pay you to keep the songs current for their toys. The jar is not that fee.

Leave the jar on for ordinary visits. Turn it off when the visit carries their tag, so a parent who arrived from their page is not shown a wallet. When you first write to them, do not mention the jar. If they ask how the page stays up, say the songs are free and there is an optional support button you will switch off for their link.

## Not this, yet

- Fixing the ear so listen mode hears a note. Worth doing for the child. Useless as a way to count finished songs until it is reliable.
- A link of your own on the paper you hand a teacher, rather than a temporary preview address. Put `?from=paper` on that QR so you can count openings from the paper, still without knowing who. Do not print the code until the address is one you will keep.
- A visit counter that stores a number and not a person.
